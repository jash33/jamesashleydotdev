It's difficult to explain where skill in a given domain comes from. Many famous
athletes and musicians who have reached the top of their field struggle to
articulate what makes them successful - often attributing success to black boxes
like talent or instinct. While the existence of talent is unquestionable, that
does not help individuals whose life demands that they develop more compentency
in a current domain than they currently have.

I will refer to a person expressing competency in this article as a "performer",
for short.

## Scenario Recognition

To be competent in a domain, a performer must correctly identify the scenario
they are contending with, and maintain their understanding of it as it evolves.
This is scenario recognition.

A performer should have a "dictionary" of scenarios, aquired either from study
or lived experience. This dictionary is a performer's knowledge of possible
scenarios they may contend with. Experience ultimately allows for the fastest,
most accurate, and nuanced understanding of the scenario at hand. The more
scenarios a performer knows, the more data points they have available to use to
identify nuance in the current one, since they rarely repeat exactly and
probably fall somewhere between existing experiences a performer has already
had.

As an example - in soccer, scenario recognition is to construct and maintain a
mental map of where players are on the field at all times, their momentum, their
intentions. If you are a software architect, it may be the entire set of a
customer's priorities, skillsets, resources, existing architecture, politics,
etc.

## Technique Vocabularly

Identifying scenarios has little use if we have no tools to deal with them. A
"technique dictionary" is a performer's tool bank of techniques and the skill to
express them. It is the vocabularly of a performer.

In soccer, this might be certain types of shots, passes, dribbles, and the skill
to reliably perform them. If you are a software achitect, this might be knowing
a variety of databases, object stores, caches, queues, scaling techniques, and
the tradeoffs of every technology and configuration.It could also be the soft
skills needed to guide a conversation and frame goals in terms of the key
decision maker's priorities.

## Pattern Matching

The next aspect of competency is the joining of the scenario and technique
dictionaries to apply the appropriate technique to the appropriate scenario. The
perfect match considers every nuance of the scenario as well as every side
effect of the technique.

## Social Multipliers

The final aspect of competency is how particular expressions of it become a
multiplying force. Most of us are familiar with someone who is highly competent
but is not well liked. That person limits their own value by stripping their
competency of its potential for wide influence. If competency is expressed in a
way that can be shared with others, in a way that respects others as having
dignity, emotions, and varied experiences, then that competency (which is
invaluable, having been hard-won across potentially decades) becomes something
that can be shared and postively influence others. In a large organization, this
can have massive, wide-ranging effects, even establishing cultures.
